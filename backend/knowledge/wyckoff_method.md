## PART 1: THE THREE WYCKOFF LAWS

### Law 1: Supply and Demand
**Core principle:** Price rises when demand exceeds supply; price falls when supply exceeds demand; price moves sideways when supply and demand are in balance.

**Operational interpretation:**
- Rising price on increasing volume = demand overwhelming supply (bullish)
- Rising price on decreasing volume = demand weakening; trend is losing energy (warning)
- Falling price on increasing volume = supply overwhelming demand (bearish)
- Falling price on decreasing volume = supply exhausting; downtrend losing energy (opportunity)

**Volume is the fuel. Price is the car.**
If the car accelerates but fuel consumption drops — the move is not sustainable.

### Law 2: Cause and Effect
**Core principle:** A cause must be built before an effect (trend move) can occur. The size of the cause determines the magnitude of the effect.

**Operational interpretation:**
- A wide accumulation range (large cause) → large upside move (large effect)
- A narrow consolidation (small cause) → small breakout move (small effect)
- Distribution over many weeks/months → deep downside move

**Point and Figure counting:**  
Wyckoff practitioners use horizontal P&F counts to project targets from accumulation/distribution ranges. For Fibrios: the wider the trading range, the more significant the breakout target.

### Law 3: Effort vs. Result
**Core principle:** Volume (effort) should produce proportional price movement (result). When effort and result diverge, the trend is weakening.

**Divergence patterns:**
| Condition | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| High volume, small price bar | Supply/Demand absorption — professionals trading against the move |
| Low volume, wide price bar | No resistance — trend continuation likely |
| Volume climax + wide bar + immediate reversal | Selling/Buying climax — trend exhaustion |
| Volume declining on rallies | Distribution — professionals unloading |
| Volume increasing on dips | Accumulation — professionals absorbing supply |

## PART 2: THE COMPOSITE OPERATOR (CO)

**Definition:** The "Composite Operator" is Wyckoff's concept of the aggregate behavior of large institutional participants — banks, funds, market makers — acting with common financial interest (not coordination).

**The CO's operating cycle:**
1. **Accumulate** large positions at low prices (drive price down to get cheap fills)
2. **Mark up** — allow/encourage price rise through momentum phases
3. **Distribute** large positions at high prices (drive price up to get expensive fills from retail)
4. **Mark down** — allow/encourage price decline; repeat

**How the CO creates liquidity:**
- Before accumulation: CO *drives price down* below support to trigger retail stop-losses → creates supply of cheap shares/contracts
- Before distribution: CO *drives price up* above resistance to trigger retail breakout buyers → creates demand at expensive levels (retail buys what CO is selling)

**Fibrios connection:** ICT's "liquidity sweeps" and "stop hunts" are the *same mechanism* as Wyckoff's CO creating liquidity. Wyckoff provides the macro structural context (phase); ICT provides the micro execution (sweep pattern).

## PART 3: ACCUMULATION SCHEMATIC

### Overview
Accumulation occurs after a downtrend. The CO absorbs supply over an extended range before marking price up.

```
     PRICE
       |
 PS ───┤                                           MARKUP begins →
       |                                          /
 SC ───┤  (selling climax, volume spike)         /
       |    \_____                           ____/
 AR ───┤          \    BC  LPS             /  ← SOS
       |           \___/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\___/
 ST ───┤                   SPRING
       |           ↑ (Test — low volume)
       └──────────────────────────────────────────→ TIME
                   PHASE A  │ PHASE B │ PHASE C │ PHASE D │ PHASE E
```

### Phase A — Stopping the Downtrend

**PS (Preliminary Support):** First sign that buying is beginning to appear after extended downtrend. Volume increases, wider price spread to the upside. Not yet a reversal — just first resistance to decline.

**SC (Selling Climax):** The panic bottom. Characterized by:
- Maximum selling pressure — highest volume in recent memory
- Wide down bar closing near lows
- Often a spike down that is immediately recovered (long wick)
- Institutions begin heavy absorption of all supply

**AR (Automatic Rally):** Sharp bounce from SC low. Supply has been temporarily exhausted. Price rises quickly on decreasing volume. The AR high establishes the *top of the trading range*.

**ST (Secondary Test):** Price returns to SC area on lower volume to test supply. Confirms the SC was a climax. If volume is significantly lower than SC → accumulation confirmed.

**Trading Range (TR):** Defined by SC low and AR high. All of Phase B-D occurs within or around this range.

### Phase B — Building the Cause

**Duration:** Can last weeks to months. This is where the CO does the heavy lifting — absorbing all available supply.

**Characteristics:**
- Multiple tests of both ends of the trading range
- Volume tends to be higher on down moves toward SC low, lower on up moves
- Price action appears choppy and directionless — **intentionally** so (the CO needs time to accumulate)
- Occasional Upthrust (UT): brief spike above AR high on low volume — tests supply above range; if supply appears, price falls back quickly (bearish for immediate term; neutral for accumulation)

**Fibrios identification in Phase B:**
- Equal highs and equal lows within the range → liquidity pools (ICT alignment)
- Order blocks at repeated test levels → institutional accumulation footprint
- Volume declining on tests of range lows → absorption

### Phase C — The Test (Spring)

**Spring:** The most critical event in accumulation. Price breaks below the SC low (below the trading range) — seemingly resuming the downtrend — then immediately recovers.

**Purpose of the Spring:**
- Triggers stop-losses of longs who bought in Phase B → creates fresh supply
- CO absorbs this final supply at maximum discount
- Tests whether supply is truly exhausted

**Characteristics of a valid Spring:**
- Volume is LOW (no real supply at these prices — confirming absorption is complete)
- Price recovers quickly above SC low — often within 1-2 candles
- Wide bar up from the spring low

**Failed Spring (No Supply Confirmed):** Volume extremely low on the Spring → the market has been absorbed. Next move is markup.

**Test of the Spring:** Often, price will come back to test the Spring low on even lower volume before marking up. This is the final confirmation.

**Fibrios connection:** The Spring = ICT's liquidity sweep below a swing low + immediate reversal. The Wyckoff framework explains WHY the sweep happens (CO collecting final supply).

### Phase D — Trend Establishment

**SOS (Sign of Strength):** First strong up-move breaking above Phase B resistance, on high volume. Confirms the CO has completed accumulation and is now marking up.

**LPS (Last Point of Support):** Pullback to former resistance (now support) after SOS. Volume decreases on the pullback. This is the **optimal long entry** in Wyckoff analysis.

**Characteristics of Phase D:**
- Rallies are wide, strong bars on high volume
- Pullbacks are narrow, weak bars on low volume
- Price holds above SC/AR area — old resistance becomes support

**Fibrios signal:** SOS = Break of Structure (BOS) in ICT language. LPS = retest of the broken level = demand zone. The system can align ICT BOS + Wyckoff LPS for highest-probability entries.

### Phase E — Markup

Price trends up, away from the trading range. Corrections are shallow. Volume supports the uptrend. The Wyckoff cycle is complete.

## PART 4: DISTRIBUTION SCHEMATIC

### Overview
Distribution occurs after an uptrend. The CO unloads accumulated positions to retail buyers at high prices before marking price down.

```
     PRICE
       |
       |    MARKDOWN begins →
 PSY ──┤  ___                                         \
       | /   \                                         \
 BC ───┤/  UT \   UT again?   SOW                      \
       |       \___/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\___________________\
       |             LPSY   LPSY
 AR ───┤
       └──────────────────────────────────────────────────→ TIME
                PHASE A  │ PHASE B │ PHASE C │ PHASE D  │ PHASE E
```

### Phase A — Stopping the Uptrend

**PSY (Preliminary Supply):** First sign of supply entering after an extended uptrend. Volume increases, bars become wider. Prices still advance but momentum slows.

**BC (Buying Climax):** The panic top. Maximum buying pressure:
- Highest volume in recent history on an up move
- Wide up bar (often a shooting star or wick to upper extreme)
- CO is selling aggressively into all this demand
- Price immediately reverses

**AR (Automatic Reaction):** Sharp drop after BC. Demand exhausted temporarily. Sets the *bottom of the trading range*.

**ST (Secondary Test):** Price returns to BC area on lower volume. If volume and spread are less than BC → confirms the BC was a climax; distribution underway.

### Phase B — Building the Cause (Distribution)

**Characteristics:**
- Multiple tests of trading range boundaries
- Upthrusts (UT): brief pushes above BC high on high volume, then reversal → CO selling into breakout buyers
- Volume higher on down moves, lower on up moves (opposite of accumulation)
- Price appears to be in a bull consolidation to retail traders

**Key events:**
- **UT (Upthrust):** Price breaks above BC high, triggers breakout buyers. CO sells into this demand. Price rapidly returns below BC high. Volume may or may not be high.
- **mUT (minor Upthrust):** Smaller version within the range

### Phase C — The Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD)

**UTAD:** Price breaks above the entire trading range, then reverses and falls back below it. Mirror image of the Spring.

**Purpose:**
- Triggers stop-losses of shorts + triggers breakout longs → CO absorbs all demand at maximum premium
- Tests for demand at these levels — finds very little genuine demand

**Characteristics of a valid UTAD:**
- Volume may be high (breakout buyers all enter) but price quickly reverses
- Price cannot hold above the BC/UT high
- Reversal is sharp and immediate

**Fibrios connection:** UTAD = ICT's buy-side liquidity sweep above equal highs. After the sweep, price dumps — the "bull trap."

### Phase D — Trend Establishment (Bearish)

**SOW (Sign of Weakness):** First strong down-move breaking below Phase B support, on high volume. Confirms distribution is complete.

**LPSY (Last Point of Supply):** Rally back to former support (now resistance) after SOW. Volume is LOW on the rally — no genuine demand. This is the **optimal short entry** in Wyckoff analysis.

**Characteristics:**
- Drops are wide, strong bars on high volume
- Rallies are narrow, weak on low volume
- Price holds below BC/AR area — old support becomes resistance

**Fibrios signal:** SOW = Break of Structure (BOS) to downside in ICT. LPSY = retest of broken support level = supply zone. ICT supply zone + Wyckoff LPSY = highest-probability short.

### Phase E — Markdown

Price trends down away from the distribution range. Rallies are shallow. The Wyckoff cycle is complete.

## PART 5: VOLUME ANALYSIS AT KEY WYCKOFF EVENTS

### 5.1 Volume Interpretation Rules

| Event | Expected Volume | Low Volume Meaning | High Volume Meaning |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| SC / BC | Very high | — (climax must be high) | Confirmation of climax |
| Spring | Very low | Absorption complete — BUY | Supply still present — WAIT |
| UTAD | High initially | — | Breakout trap — CO selling |
| SOS | High | Weak — may fail | Strong — markup confirmed |
| LPS | Low | Absorption — optimal entry | Still volatile — wait |
| SOW | High | — | Markdown confirmed |
| LPSY | Low | No demand — optimal short | Hidden demand — wait |

### 5.2 Effort vs. Result Application
- **Up bar, high volume, but closes in lower 1/3:** Supply absorbed the demand. Bearish.
- **Down bar, high volume, but closes in upper 1/3:** Demand absorbed the supply. Bullish.
- **Narrow bar after wide move:** Momentum exhausting.
- **Wide bar no volume:** Vacuum move — not sustainable; will be retested.

## PART 7: WYCKOFF SCORING FOR FIBRIOS ANALYSIS

When identifying market structure, apply Wyckoff phase scoring:

### 7.1 Phase Identification Score
| Confirmed Event | Points |
|----------------|--------|
| SC/BC confirmed (volume climax + reversal) | +25 |
| AR confirmed (bounce/drop from climax) | +10 |
| ST confirmed (secondary test at lower volume) | +10 |
| Spring/UTAD confirmed (low volume sweep + reversal) | +35 |
| Test of Spring/UTAD confirmed | +20 |
| SOS/SOW confirmed (BOS with volume) | +25 |
| LPS/LPSY in progress (low-vol pullback to BOS) | +30 |

### 7.2 Trade Entry Quality by Phase
| Phase | Entry Quality | Action |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| Phase A — Climax | Poor (timing unknown) | Identify only — no trade yet |
| Phase B — Chop | Low | Wait — no edge yet |
| Phase C — Spring/UTAD | Very High | IMMEDIATE signal — best entry |
| Phase D — LPS/LPSY | High | Best-risk entry after confirmation |
| Phase E — Markup/Markdown | Medium | Trend continuation; watch for next distribution |
